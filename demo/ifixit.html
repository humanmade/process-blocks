Follow this guide to replace a broken screen on a MacBook Neo. The screen also includes the integrated cables for the display and camera.

For your safety, discharge the battery below 25% before disassembly. This reduces the risk of a dangerous fire if the battery is accidentally damaged during the repair. If your battery is swollen, take appropriate precautions.

Difficulty: Moderate · Time required: 45 minutes - 1 hour

Tools

  • Spudger
  • P5 Pentalobe Bit
  • 3IP Torx Plus Bit
  • 5IP Torx Plus Bit
  • 8IP Torx Plus Bit
  • iFixit Precision Bit Driver
  • Angled Tweezers
  • iFixit Opening Picks (Set of 6)

Guide

  • ● Fully shut down the MacBook and close the lid. Keep the lid closed until you've physically disconnected the battery.
  • Disconnect any cables and accessories.
  • Flip the computer upside‑down on a clean work surface.
  • Use a P5 pentalobe driver to remove the eight screws securing the bottom cover:
    • ● Six 4.1 mm‑long screws
    • ● Two 4.5 mm‑long screws
  • Reminder: During reassembly, partially install the screws to ensure alignment, then tighten the screws at each corner (like top‑right, bottom‑left, etc.) before the rest.
  • Note: The bottom cover is held in place with two clips, one on each side.
  • Use your fingers to lift the top right corner of the bottom cover, near the USB‑C ports, until the clip along the right edge releases.
    • Note: If you're having trouble, use the flat end of a spudger to pry up the corner until you can grab it with your fingers. You can also slide an opening pick down each edge to release the clips.
  • Repeat this process on the left side to release its clip.
  • Remove the bottom cover.
  • Reminder: During reassembly, align the bottom cover with its screw holes, then press on the two short sides until the clip on each side snaps into place.
  • ● Use a Torx Plus 3IP driver to remove the two 1.7 mm‑long screws securing the battery connector cover.
    • Note: While the MacBook Neo uses Torx Plus screws, standard Torx bits also work (T3 in place of 3IP, for example). Apply constant, downward force to prevent stripping the screw.
  • Use tweezers or your fingers to remove the battery connector cover.
  • Use the flat end of a spudger to pry up and disconnect the battery press connector.
    • Reminder: To reconnect, align the connector carefully over its socket and press down—first on one of the short sides, then the other—until it clicks into place. Don't try to force the connector into place. If you're having trouble, reposition it and try again.
  • ● Use a Torx Plus 3IP driver to remove the 1.7 mm‑long screw securing the antenna connector cover.
  • Use tweezers or your fingers to remove the antenna connector cover.
  • Insert one arm of angled tweezers under the metal neck of one of the antenna coaxial connectors and lift straight up to disconnect it.
  • Repeat the process to disconnect the second antenna cable.
  • Reminder: To reconnect, use tweezers to hold each connector in place over its socket and gently press down with your finger or a spudger—the connector should "snap" into place. If you're having trouble, reposition the head and try again.
  • ● Use a Torx Plus 5IP driver to remove the four 2.9 mm‑long screws securing the antenna.
  • Pick up the antenna by its edges and remove it.
  • Use the flat end of a spudger to gently push under the metal buckle of the display cable connector, located on the top‑right corner of the logic board, to unclip it.
  • Grab the buckle and cable together and gently pull the connector straight out of its socket.
  • Repeat this procedure to disconnect the camera cable, located on the left edge of the logic board.
  • Reminder: To reconnect:
    • Grip the buckle and insert the connector fully into its socket. Realign it as necessary and don't try to force it in.
    • Flip the buckle over the socket and press it down until it clicks into place.
  • Pick up the buckle and cable together and gently pull the camera cable out of its channel securing it to the frame.
  • Free the cable so it's not wrapped around the corner of the laptop frame.
  • Hold the display cable's buckle and cable together and free it from the laptop frame.
  • ● Use a Torx Plus 8IP driver to loosen (not remove) all four hinge screws.
  • Reminder: During reassembly:
    • Loosen the hinge screws at least one full turn.
    • Adjust the screen until it's correctly centered and aligned on each side. The screen should sit flush with the rest of the laptop.
      • Note: You can stand the laptop up on one side to help align the screen. Support it with one hand while tightening the screws with the other.
    • Fully tighten the four hinge screws.
    • Note: If the screen clicks or snaps upon opening, loosen the hinge screws and adjust the alignment. Make sure the screen doesn't sit too far forward, or it can catch on the body when opening and closing.
    • Note: If desired, add a little blue threadlocker to the screw threads to prevent them from loosening over time.
  • Open the laptop all the way and gently place it keyboard side down on a table, so the screen hangs over the edge.
    • Note: You can place a soft cloth under the keyboard to protect it.
  • Caution: Keep a firm grip on the laptop, as it can easily fall off the table.
  • Support the screen with one hand.
  • ● With your free hand, use a Torx Plus 8IP driver to remove the four 6.2 mm–long screws securing the hinges.
  • Hold the screen with both hands and lift the hinges out of their recesses to remove it.
  • Reminder: During reassembly, be careful not to scratch the new screen on the edge of the laptop frame. If the screen itself has any plastic liners, leave them in place until reassembly is complete.

Compare the new replacement part to the original part—you may need to remove plastic liners from the new part before you install it.

To reassemble the MacBook, follow these instructions in reverse order.
