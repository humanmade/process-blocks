Flat tyres happen. This guide takes you through replacing an inner tube at the roadside or at home, in three stages.

If you're working on the rear wheel, shift into the smallest rear cog. This moves the derailleur out of the way and makes removing the wheel much easier.

For rim brakes, open the quick-release lever on the caliper so the tyre can pass between the pads. Disc brakes need no adjustment, but don't squeeze the brake lever while the wheel is out.

Open the quick-release skewer or unscrew the thru-axle, then lift the bike and let the wheel drop out.

Remove the valve cap and press the valve core to let out any remaining air.

Opposite the valve, hook a tyre lever under the bead and pry it over the rim. Hook it to a spoke, insert a second lever a few centimetres along, and slide it around the rim.

Pull out the old tube. Carefully run your fingers around the inside of the tyre to find whatever caused the puncture, and remove it. Skipping this step usually means another flat within minutes.

Inflate the new tube just enough to give it shape. Insert the valve through the rim, then tuck the tube into the tyre all the way around.

Starting at the valve, push the bead back over the rim with your thumbs, working around both sides until the last section pops in. Avoid using levers here, as they can pinch the new tube.

Pump the tyre to the pressure printed on its sidewall, checking that the bead sits evenly all the way around.

Drop the wheel back into the dropouts (for the rear, guide the chain over the smallest cog), then close the skewer or tighten the thru-axle. Close the brake quick-release.

Spin the wheel to make sure it runs true and doesn't rub, then squeeze the brakes a few times before riding.
